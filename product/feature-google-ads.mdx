This is the living spec for **Paid Search** — the feature that lets a small-business Blaze user connect Google Ads and launch a real Search campaign. It summarizes the product; the authoritative blueprint is the Google Ads engineering spec (**BLZ-12719**) in Linear, and the PRD lives in Blaze. When this page and those disagree, treat Linear and the PRD as the source of record and open a PR to fix whichever is wrong.

## Overview

Paid Search lets a Blaze user connect their **Google Ads** account over OAuth and run an AI-guided wizard that builds and launches a real Google Ads Search campaign. Once a campaign is live, you manage it from a steady-state dashboard. The feature is aimed at small-business users who want to advertise without learning the Google Ads console.

The feature is largely shipped in beta — 65 of 74 tickets are done. The older UI names it "Google Ads"; the newer steady-state UI names it "Paid Search."

## The creation wizard

The wizard walks you through six steps, from picking a goal to launching a campaign. The current step is tracked by the `onboarding_step` enum: `goal → business → keywords → copy → details → connect → launched`.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Goal">
    Pick one objective: **Leads**, **Calls**, **Sales**, or **Bookings**. The conversion event type is derived from the goal you choose.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Business context">
    Describe the business so the AI has something to work from: service description, geography, customer type, differentiators, offer text and deadline, business hours (set per day, defaulting to Mon–Fri 9am–6pm), campaign categories (multi-select), and target languages (multi-select).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keywords">
    The AI generates target keywords and negative keywords, using Google Keyword Planner plus LLM filtering against your business info. Target keywords appear as editable chips, each with a match-type toggle (**Exact**, **Phrase**, or **Broad**). Negatives are a reviewable list. You can add your own custom keywords.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy">
    The LLM generates Responsive Search Ad copy: six headline sets, one per angle — intent match, USP, offer/promotion, social proof, call to action, and aspiration — with 2–3 headlines each, plus one description set of four descriptions. You can edit and approve each item, regenerate a whole angle, and see a live ad preview. Headlines follow Google's rules: minimum 3, maximum 15, and a 30-character cap on each. The launch gate for this step is that every one of the six headline angle-groups has at least one approved headline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Details and budget">
    Set a daily budget, with an AI budget estimate to guide you — estimated monthly conversions, cost per conversion, and a recommended daily range from Keyword Planner. Set a start date, an optional end date, and an audience size (**Small**, **Medium**, or **Large**).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect">
    Connect Google Ads over OAuth. You can skip this step if an integration already exists. Two verification checks run after OAuth before launch is allowed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Post-OAuth verification

Two checks run after you connect Google Ads:

1. **Conversion tracking** — verifies that at least one conversion action has recorded a conversion in the last 30 days. This is a hard gate: if it fails, launch is blocked.
2. **Existing campaigns** — if the account already has active campaigns, you decide whether Blaze manages them. V1 proceeds in new-campaign mode, running alongside the existing campaigns.

## Launch

Launch is a two-phase campaign build that runs as a background job. On success, the campaign is set active. On failure, it's set to error and reported to Sentry.

### Phase 1 — Non-brand Search (always)

Blaze builds a non-brand Search campaign with:

- **Maximize Conversions** bidding.
- Search Partners off and Display off.
- Presence-only location targeting.
- A business-hours ad schedule.
- A −100% tablet bid adjustment.
- Exact and Phrase match keywords, with audience observation.
- Negative keywords.
- A Responsive Search Ad built from the approved headlines, display paths, sitelinks, callouts, and a structured snippet.

### Phase 2 — Brand Search (conditional)

If brand keyword volume exists — checked via Keyword Planner — Blaze also creates a **Maximize Clicks** campaign on brand keywords, with non-brand negatives.

## Steady state

Once a campaign is live, you manage it from a dashboard.

- **Campaigns list and detail.** The detail view shows KPIs, a 14-day CTR chart against an industry benchmark, an ad-groups table, and the campaign type and bid strategy.
- **Health-status pills.** A campaign shows one of: **Live**, **On track**, **Testing**, **Winner**, **Spending too fast**, **Paused**, or **Over budget**.
- **Actions.** Rename, pause/resume, edit, or delete a campaign (deleting also deletes it in Google Ads), and remove an ad.
- **Reporting.** A weekly report and a Paid Search insights page are exposed.
- **Timeframe filtering.** Filter by 7, 14, 30, or 90 days or lifetime, with period-over-period comparison.

Metrics use the shared **MetricSnapshot** platform-insights system, with one snapshot per day. Imported and connected existing campaigns are backfilled.

## Where we're headed

The goal is for Blaze to run Google Search advertising end to end — launching and managing campaigns, keywords, copy, budgets, and conversion tracking — with AI taking over the repetitive optimization work. The shipped wizard is the first slice; the sections below are the broader target and the automation layer we're building toward, not features that are all live today.

### Target management capabilities

Beyond launching a campaign, Blaze is building toward the full ongoing-management surface an advertiser needs:

- **Launch new campaigns** — set campaign type, objective, budget, bid strategy, and geo/schedule targeting.
- **Manage keywords** — add keywords, set match types (broad, phrase, exact), pause low performers, and adjust keyword-level bids in manual or hybrid setups.
- **Manage search terms and negatives** — mine the search-terms report, add negative keywords and negative lists to cut wasted spend, and promote high-intent queries to keywords.
- **Write and manage ad copy** — create and refresh responsive search ads, pin assets where needed, and retire low-strength or low-CTR ads.
- **Manage assets and extensions** — sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, images, and lead forms at the right account, campaign, or ad-group level.
- **Adjust budgets and bidding** — raise or lower daily budgets, shift spend between campaigns, and tune the bid strategy (Max Conversions, tCPA, tROAS, Max Clicks, manual CPC).
- **Pause and prune losers** — turn off underperforming keywords, ads, ad groups, or campaigns based on CPA, ROAS, conversion volume, or quality signals.
- **Optimize Performance Max** — manage asset groups, audience signals, listing groups, and brand exclusions, and use the limited PMax reporting to steer the black box.
- **Set up and verify conversion tracking** — configure conversion actions, import from GA4, CRM, or offline sources, set up Enhanced Conversions, and confirm data is flowing for the bidding algorithm.
- **Adjust targeting and audiences** — geo, device, ad schedule, and demographic adjustments, plus audience signals, remarketing lists, and customer match, with bid adjustments where the strategy allows.

Today the shipped wizard already covers new-campaign creation, keyword match types, AI ad copy, negatives, budget and bid strategy, and targeting for Search. Ongoing management — search-terms mining, editing existing campaigns, and PMax/Demand Gen/Shopping — is planned.

On campaign types: **Search** is what's live today, **Performance Max** is the next planned type, and **Demand Gen** and **Shopping** are further-out goals.

### AI automations (roughly by time saved)

The automation layer is the differentiator: Blaze aims to do the repetitive optimization work an advertiser would otherwise do by hand. This is the roadmap and vision — the items below are prioritized by time saved, and most are not yet shipped.

1. **Search-terms mining and negative-keyword automation** — any account paying for clicks generates thousands of queries a month, and reviewing them is hours of painstaking work; auto-add waste to the negative-keyword list.
2. **Automated reporting and narrative** — the biggest recurring time sink, and it scales with book size; generate the report and the story behind it.
3. **Proactive anomaly and performance-shift detection** — watch CPA, ROAS, conversion-volume, and impression-share drift across the account and surface a triaged queue. The end state is autonomous agents handling bid adjustments, budget allocation, negative-keyword management, ad-copy testing, and anomaly detection without a human logging in.
4. **PMax / AI Max "black-box" steering** — the levers here are configuration, not execution: asset-group quality, audience signals (directional hints, not hard restrictions), search themes, brand exclusions, and dangerous defaults. An AI that audits these, flags silent auto-enrollments, and recommends signal and exclusion changes is a genuine wedge.
5. **Budget reallocation, including PMax-vs-Search cannibalization** — running PMax and Search (or Standard Shopping) together creates internal bid competition for the same products, and accounts without proper allocation reportedly waste 20–30% of spend on cannibalization; AI that detects the overlap and recommends the split is high-value and hard to do manually.
6. **RSA copy generation and automated ad testing** — generation is commoditized, so the differentiation is the testing engine that continuously monitors RSA performance, finds statistically significant winners and losers, and recommends which headlines and descriptions to keep, pause, or replace.
7. **Conversion-tracking and Enhanced Conversions verification** — automated bidding is only as good as its inputs, so verify conversion actions fire, Enhanced Conversions is configured, and offline/CRM imports are flowing.
8. **Bulk campaign and ad-group buildout** — from a brief or by cloning a winner; a launch-heavy-week time saver.
9. **Account-structure and wasted-spend audits** — a periodic audit for cannibalization, redundant negatives, learning-phase issues, and structural cleanup.
10. **Onboarding and new-account audit automation** — run the full audit on access grant and produce a prioritized plan.

One design question is still open: whether negative keywords should be added at the campaign or account level.
